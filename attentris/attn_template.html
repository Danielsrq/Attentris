Attention over tokens (mean of 4 heads)

The heat is the selected candidate's cross-attention over the board tokens, drawn through the token masks. Per-cell density divides each token's share by its size, so a 4-cell T slot and the 100-cell sky compare fairly; whole-token share paints every cell of a token with its full share. Overlapping overlays add up. Dashed outline: the move the bot played. Solid blue: the candidate selected above. Hover a token row to outline it. Attention shows where information was routed from, not proof of what decided the move.

Why it looked there. Each attention score is the sum of two terms (shown per token, mean of the 4 heads, in logit units): geom. is the FLAT-2D bias, a learned function of geometry only (how the move's 4 cells and box sit relative to the token's mask and box: overlap and row/column offsets); content is Q·K/√d, what the token's vector says to the move's. The heat-map switch shows the attention each term alone would give (softmax over the tokens of that term): geometry only is where a move would look from position alone, content only is where it would look from meaning alone. A token high in the real attention but not in the geometry-only map was chosen for its content. The geom. and content columns and the signed view are shown relative to the mean over this move's board tokens (piece tokens, marked *, are shown raw): softmax ignores adding the same constant to every score, so only differences between tokens carry meaning (hover a number for its raw value). Heads: "all" averages the 4 heads, but in two different places: the signed view averages the biases, while the attention and the geometry-only / content-only maps average the softmaxed shares, so when heads disagree the two can differ. Pick one head to see it on its own; the board, the token list and the bars then all show that head.
