The weights found

The bot scores a board as a weighted sum of these measurements. Each weight is in lines of attack per typical amount of its feature (for example, per 10 holes or per 40 row transitions), so the numbers are comparable. Negative weights are penalties. Sorted by size; the first 12 drive almost all of the play.

CEM or CMA-ES at this budget?

The same two algorithms on standard test functions in 29 dimensions, starting 2.0 from the optimum, with and without 10% fitness noise. Each cell is the median final value over repeated runs; lower is better. CMA-ES needs enough generations to learn its covariance.

Trajectory

The weights live in 29 dimensions, so each panel shows one flat slice through them. Horizontal axis: the straight line from the starting weights (0) to where the run ended up; moving right changes many weights at once, in the mix listed under the panel. Vertical axis: the other direction the candidates varied most, also listed. Colour: fitness (APP) measured on a grid of weight settings in that slice, darker is better. Dots are the candidates a generation tried (filled = the winners kept), each ellipse is that generation's search cloud, and the black line is the cloud's centre moving from start to finish.

One weight at a time

Start from the tuned normal-rules weights, move one weight up and down (three steps each way) and keep the rest fixed. Each chart shows the resulting fitness; the open dot is the tuned value. All charts share one vertical scale, and Δ is how much fitness changed across the range. Changes below about 0.05 APP are within the noise of the 12 test games, so those weights are listed separately: moving them alone does not change how well the bot plays.

Plane through three solutions

Three sets of weights fix one flat slice through weight space: the starting weights (bottom left), the earlier CEM result with normalised features, and the one with raw features. Colour is fitness at each point of that slice, darker is better. The question it answers: did the two tunes find the same hill, or two different ones? Axis units are the distance from the start to the normalised result.

Shape of the search cloud

How spread out each run's search cloud was, generation by generation (left to right). Each row is one of the cloud's 29 principal directions, widest at the top; darker means more spread. CEM's cloud shrinks as it settles; CMA-ES's stays wide. The bar charts show which weights make up CMA-ES's narrowest direction (where small changes hurt fitness most) and its widest (where the weights hardly matter).
